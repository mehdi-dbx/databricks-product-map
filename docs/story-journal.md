# Story journal

Decisions taken while running the three Story tasks: the starting situation, the chapters and the cement. Each entry gives the decision, the reason, and the option set aside. The run started on 2026-10-11.

## Run setup

- **The per-chapter review gates are suspended.** The guide and the prompts say to wait for a verdict after each chapter. For this run, the work goes straight through and is reviewed once, at the end, in the app.
  - Reason: the instruction was to run all three tasks without stopping.
- **The 38 points of chapters 2–11 are written by three parallel agents.** Each agent follows `docs/story-editorial-guide.md`, for chapters 2–4, 5–7 and 8–11. Each one writes a temporary file and validates it with `apply_story_additions.py --check`. The main session reviews every item, then applies them.
  - Reason: the guide is self-contained, and the agents can write while the starting situation is being done.
  - Set aside: writing the 38 points one after the other in the main session.
- **The guide's three examples (7.2, 8.1, 9.1) are reused as they are.**
  - Reason: they were already reviewed, and their sources were checked.

## Task 1 — Starting situation

- **`docs/story-pains.md` was generated by a script.** It holds 11 chapters, 40 points, 59 capsule references and 258 pains. 10 capsule references have no pains in the tree.
- **The pains were grouped into 8 challenges, one point each, in the order of the chapters that answer them.** The map is 1.1 → ch. 2, 1.2 → ch. 3, 1.3 → ch. 4, 1.4 → ch. 5, 1.5 → ch. 6–7, 1.6 → ch. 8–9, 1.7 → ch. 10 and 1.8 → ch. 11.
  - Reason: a complete synthesis that the cement can close point by point.
  - Set aside: 5–6 broader groups, because they would have merged chapters that solve different problems, such as data science (ch. 3) and model access (ch. 4).
- **The former points 1.1 and 1.2 keep every sentence.**
  - 1.1 gains two sentences: hand-built pipelines and stale copies, then nobody warned when a table breaks.
  - 1.2 becomes 1.8, the last point, and gains one sentence on models that are hard to run in production. Its "So AI projects start as pilots…" now follows the link "With data, models and tools in this state…".
  - Reason: the pilots point sums up every earlier group, and the handoff "the work starts with the data" then leads back to chapter 2.
- **The link 1.1 → 1.2 is kept word for word.** "AI needs that data…" also leads to model work.
- **Point 1.6 calls back to 1.1 ("those few colleagues") instead of repeating it.** 1.1 already says that people wait for the few colleagues who can query the systems.
- **No sentence of chapter 1 repeats a later lead word for word.** The sentence "a feature computed one way for training and another way in production gives wrong predictions" is the lead of 3.2, so 1.2 says "features are often computed differently for training and in production" instead.
- **Chapter 1 goes from 2 points and 9 bullets to 8 points and 32 bullets.** That is the price of a complete synthesis. The cement opening sums it up in one paragraph.
- **`docs/story-cement.md` no longer relies on "the four obstacles of point 1.2".** Its inputs, opening, closing and checks now use the points of chapter 1 and the Check M1 map.

## Task 2 — Chapters

- **The agents' output was rejected, and the 38 points were written in the main session.**
  - Chapters 2–4: fragments of the source pasted as sentences ("ACID transactions.") and formula pivots ("X addresses this").
  - Chapters 5–7: bullets restated (5.3, 5.5, 6.1, 6.3, 7.1), sentence fragments (5.4), and the numbers of the DASF note.
  - Chapters 8–11: bullets restated (8.2, 8.5, 10.1, 11.1) and an invented duration ("weeks").
  - The agents' files stay in /tmp. They are not used.
- **Sources are given as field indexes, and a script copies their text from the trees.** The texts cannot be mistyped, and `--check` validated every item.
- **7.2, 8.1 and 9.1 are the guide's examples, unchanged.**
- **3.1, 7.3, 11.3 and 11.4 get no sentence. Their `report` says why.**
  - None of their capsules has `solves` rows, so a challenge would have nothing to rest on.
  - A pivot answers a challenge, so without a challenge it would only restate the bullets.
  - An effect drawn from the note would repeat the bullets or the lead. For 7.3 the only facts are the DASF numbers, and they are already a bullet.
- **No added sentence repeats chapter 1 word for word.** The detail chapters echo chapter 1, but with different wording. Examples: 2.5's challenge, 5.1 ("Running a model over company data…"), 10.1 ("Each system holds its own part of the customer…").
- **A check script looked for digits, "X:" formulas, buzzwords, and sentences too close to their own lead and bullets or to chapter 1.**
  - It raised 8 flags. Seven were kept on purpose: a product name (MLflow 3), short pivots that name a product the first bullet introduces (6.3, 8.2, 10.2, 9.1), and echoes of chapter 1 with a different meaning (7.1, 8.4).
  - The real repeat was fixed. Chapter 1, point 1.5 now says "Nobody has a central view of how AI is used", so it no longer anticipates the 7.2 challenge word for word.
- **Applied with `apply_story_additions.py`.** 34 points were written, 4 had nothing to write, and none was refused. The diff of `story-notes.md` shows additions only, and a second run reported everything as already applied.

## Task 3 — Cement

- **The complete story was written in the main session, following `docs/story-cement.md`.** It has a title, an opening, a paragraph for each chapter from 2 to 11, and a closing.
- **The handoff questions were checked by a script.** Every paragraph ends on the last sentence of its chapter's handoff, word for word, and the opening ends on chapter 1's. Chapter 11 has no handoff and leads into the closing.
- **The product names were checked by a script too.** Every product a paragraph names is cited by its chapter's points, as a capsule or in the bullets. Unity Catalog, in the chapter 2 paragraph, is cited by the first bullet of 2.5 rather than by a capsule.
- **The text is 1,104 words, against about 800 in the prompt.**
  - Reason: each paragraph has 4 or 5 sentences and names its chapter's products, which comes to about 85 words per chapter. Getting down to 800 would mean dropping products or challenges.
  - Set aside: cutting the products down to one per chapter.
- **The closing names the chapters by their titles (the data foundation, the data science lifecycle…), not by their numbers.**
  - Reason: the text is told aloud. It follows the Check M1 map in order.
- **The opening ends on "The work starts with the data. How do you get it into one place…", as chapter 1's handoff does.**
- **The cement is shown where it was announced before the run:**
  - the title and the opening at the top of the Story;
  - each paragraph under its chapter's heading;
  - the closing as a last section, with a "Closing" entry in the chapter list.
  - The search covers the intros, the opening and the closing.
  - Known effect: each intro ends on the question that the chapter's handoff asks again at the end of the chapter, so that question appears twice. This was kept as agreed.
- **`story_extract.py` now reads section M15.** It fills `thread` (title, opening, closing) and each chapter's `intro`. Without M15, `story.json` keeps its former shape.
- **Not committed, not deployed.** The result is the local HTML.
